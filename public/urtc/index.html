§ 01 — Mission

A stage for students doing real research, and a room full of people who want to hear about it.

Stanford URTC is a student-run conference where undergraduates and high school researchers present engineering and technology work through papers, posters and lightning talks. Every presenter gets written feedback from our technical chairs.

It's inspired by MIT URTC, which has run every October since 2015. We want to bring the same kind of event to the West Coast in the spring.

§ 02 — Format

One day. Six ways to share your work.

  • Fig. 2.1 · PostersCore of the day

    Poster sessions

    Most of the day happens here. Researchers stand with their work, explain it one-on-one, and talk with judges, peers and visitors in an open hall.

  • Fig. 2.2 · Papers7 min

    Paper talks

    Seven-minute talks on full research papers, followed by questions from the audience.

  • Fig. 2.3 · LightningShort form

    Lightning talks

    Very short talks, good for early results, works in progress, or a single striking finding.

  • Fig. 2.4 · KeynotePlenary

    Keynote

    A talk for everyone in the room from a researcher working at the edge of their field. Speaker TBA

  • Fig. 2.5 · JudgingLive

    Live judging & audience vote

    Judges score work during the sessions, attendees vote for their favorites, and awards are announced at the close of the day.

  • Fig. 2.6 · FeedbackEvery presenter

    Written feedback

    Every presenter gets written comments from the technical chairs, so you leave with notes to take back to your lab.

§ 03 — Impact

Why it matters.

A lot of student research never leaves the lab notebook. URTC gives it an audience, gives the people who did it a way to grow, and gives the field a look at what's coming.

For student researchersA

  1. A real conference experience

    Present to judges, peers and visitors, and answer their questions. For many presenters, this will be their first research conference.

  2. Feedback you can use

    Every presenter gets written comments from our technical chairs to take back to their lab, project or next paper.

  3. Recognition for the work

    Live judging, an audience vote and awards recognize strong research, not just polished slides.

  4. A network of peers

    Meet other students working on hard technical problems, and the mentors and researchers who support them.

For the research communityB

  1. Undergraduate work, made visible

    Student research in engineering and technology gets a dedicated stage where faculty, industry and peers can see it.

  2. A West Coast venue in the spring

    MIT URTC is held in October. Stanford URTC adds a spring venue on the West Coast, so students have another chance each year to present.

  3. A pathway into research

    High school researchers present alongside undergraduates and see what the next step in research looks like.

  4. Habits that last

    Early practice with review, feedback and presenting builds habits students carry into graduate school and industry.
